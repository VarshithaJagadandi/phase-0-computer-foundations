#### Pictures, Colors, and Sound as numbers :

seeing everything on a screen is number

#### Colors : the paint-mixing idea

**imagine :** you have 3 flashlights: red, green, blue. Each has a dimmer knob that goes from 0 (off) to 255 (full brightness). Mix the 3 lights on white wall and you can make almost any color. That’s how every screen works.

One byte (8 bulbs) holds 0 to 255, so each color’s brightness fits exactly one byte. A color is <u>3 bytes</u>: red, green, blue. this is called RGB. A single dot ( a pixel) is 3 bytes, which in hex is 6 digits. that’s where #RRGGBB comes from.

| Color | Red | Green | Blue | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pure red | 255 | 0 | 0 | `#FF0000` |
| Black (all off) | 0 | 0 | 0 | `#000000` |
| White (all full) | 255 | 255 | 255 | `#FFFFFF` |

→ Each of the 3 bytes has 256 values, so 256 x 256 x 256 = 2^24 = 16.7 million colors.

#### Pictures : a grid of dots

A photo is just a big grid of pixels, like a mosaic of tiny colored tiles. Each tile is 3 bytes.

**Example**: a small photo of 1,000 x 1,000 pixels has 1,000,000 pixels x 3 bytes = 3,000,000 bytes = about **3 MB**. This is why <u>raw</u> <u>photos</u> <u>are</u> <u>big</u>, and why your 189 KB picture from Lesson 2 must have been compressed, which means cleverly shrunk by removing repeated or unnoticed details.

#### Sound : measuring a wave, again and again

**Sound** is a *wave of moving air*. A ***microphone*** measures *“how strong that wave is”*, then turns the <u>measurement</u> <u>into</u> <u>a</u> <u>number</u>. It does this many thousands of times per second. 

For example, CD-quality sound takes 44,100 measurements every second. played back in order, the numbers recreate the wave and you hear sound.

it’s like a flipbook : a cartoon is just many still pictures shown fast, and sound is many tiny measurements played fast. This is why your 30-second ringtone was bigger than your photo, since it has many numbers every second for 30 seconds.
