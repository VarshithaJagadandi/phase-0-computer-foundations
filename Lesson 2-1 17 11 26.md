#### A tour Inside the computer

#### Why this matters ?

“cloud server” → normal computer, in a big building, with no screen. It has the same parts as laptop. we can’t protect something we can’t understand.

#### The analogy: a computer is a restaurant kitchen

picture a busy restaurant.

**CPU = the chef**

The chef does the actual work : reading the recipe step, chopping, mixing, deciding. The CPU does the same with instructions. It reads tiny step, does it, then reads the next. It does this billions of times every second. remember week 1? those steps are all in binary. The CPU is the thing that actually works on all those 0s and 1s.

**RAM = the prep counter**

The chef does not run to the warehouse for every onion. They put today’s ingredients on the counter right next to them. RAM holds what the computer is working on the counter right next to them. RAM holds what the computer is working on *right now*. It is very fast, but the counter is small. And when the restaurant closes (power off), the counter is wiped clean.

**Storage (SSD or HDD) = the pantry and warehouse**

This is where everything is kept long-term : your photos, apps and files. It is big and cheap, but slower to reach than the counter. When the restaurant closes, everything stays safe here.

**Motherboard = the kitchen floor plan and all the walkways**

It is the big main board that everything plugs into. It connects the chef, the counter, and the pantry so they can pass things to each other. It has no brain of its own. It is the roads and wiring.

**Power supply = the gas and electricity connection**

Without it, nothing works. It takes power from the wall and gives each part the right amount.

**GPU = a team of 1,000 helpers who each do one simple job**

The chef is clever but works on one thing at a time (or a few, with several cores). The GPU is like huge team that all do *simple* jobs at the same time, like coloring millions of pixels. This is why GPUs are great for graphics, video, and AI.

**Network card (NIC) = the delivery door and phone line**

This is how the computer talks to other computers : orders come in, answers go out. Remember this one for security. Almost every attack on a server arrives through this door.

**The big picture in one line**

Data lives in **storage** → gets brought to **RAM →** the **CPU** works on it → results go back to RAM or storage. The **motherboard** connects them all, the **power supply** keeps them alive, and the **network card** lets the computer talk to the world.

**Cloud and security link**

When you rent a “virtual machine” on GCP, you are choosing how many chefs (CPU cores), how big the counter is (RAM), and how big the pantry is (disk). Same kitchen, just far away. And the network card and storage are where most security work happens: who can come in through the door, and who can open the pantry.

#### **question**

you are editing a photo on your laptop and the power cuts off suddenly, before you press save. when you turn the laptop on, your edits are gone. using kitchen analogy, which part held your unsaved edits, and why did they disappear?

While you edit a photo, the photo and all your changes are **already sitting on the counter (RAM)**. The chef (CPU) keeps picking things up from the counter, changing them, and putting them back. Your edits didn’t vanish *before* reaching RAM. They vanished *from* RAM, because the counter gets wiped when the power goes off.

So the full correct picture is:

1. Open the photo: it is copied from the **pantry (storage)** to the **counter (RAM)**.
2. You edit: the chef works on it, and the changes live on the **counter (RAM)**.
3. You press **Save**: the changed version is written back to the **pantry (storage)**.
4. Power cut before step 3: the counter is wiped, the pantry still has the *old* photo. Edits gone.

This is why apps have “autosave”: they keep copying from the counter to the pantry every few minutes, just in case.
